Securing Your Garden Route Property

A practical layered security guide for Garden Route homes, businesses and holiday properties, including access, CCTV and backup power.

Featherbleu Industries · Updated 30 September 2026 · Garden Route

Garden Route properties range from permanent family homes to businesses and holiday houses that stand empty for long periods. Each needs a security plan shaped around its entrances, occupancy and local environment.

Begin with the property boundary

Check vehicle and pedestrian gates, walls, fences, lighting and vegetation. An entrance should be visible, mechanically sound and easy to use without requiring someone to wait outside. Rear boundaries next to open land deserve the same attention as the street-facing side.

Protect holiday and intermittently occupied homes

Remote cameras, app-controlled access and useful alerts help owners understand what is happening when they are away. Arrange regular physical checks, maintain the garden and avoid leaving permanent codes or uncontrolled keys with short-term visitors.

Design for power interruptions

Identify the systems that must remain available: gates, garage doors, alarms, cameras, internet equipment and selected lights. Test batteries periodically and make sure one failed power supply does not silently disable several layers of protection.

Use CCTV for verification

Cover approaches and entrances with enough detail to identify activity. Review the view at night, keep lenses clear in coastal conditions and confirm recordings are being retained. Configure alerts to focus on defined areas rather than every movement.

Manage everyday access

Give staff, contractors and guests individual codes or credentials where possible. Remove access promptly, keep remotes accounted for and use an intercom or camera to verify unexpected visitors before opening the gate.

Build strong routines

Security works best when the household or team uses it consistently. Close the gate fully, arm the alarm, report faults early and agree on what to do during an incident. Keep emergency and response numbers accessible.

Review the system each year

Properties change: plants grow, buildings are altered and people come and go. A yearly walk-through can reveal blind spots, ageing batteries, unused credentials and mechanical wear before they become urgent problems.
