5 Essential Security Tips for Your Home

Five practical ways to improve home security with CCTV, access control, gate automation, lighting and monitored alarms in the Garden Route.

Featherbleu Industries · Updated 30 September 2026 · Garden Route

Good security is layered. The strongest homes do not rely on one camera, one lock or one alarm: they combine visibility, controlled access, reliable automation and sensible daily habits.

1. Install CCTV where it answers useful questions

Start with entrances, the driveway, pedestrian gates and the routes people naturally use around the property. A camera should help you identify a person or vehicle, not simply record a wide scene. Choose suitable night performance, keep lenses clean and confirm recordings can be retrieved when needed. Remote viewing is especially useful for holiday homes and properties that stand empty for part of the year.

2. Control who can enter

Keys and shared remotes are difficult to manage once they are lost or copied. Keypads, tags, smart locks and intercoms make it easier to grant, change and remove access. Give each regular user their own credential where possible, review active users periodically and remove access as soon as it is no longer required.

3. Automate gates and garage doors safely

A dependable automated entrance reduces the time spent waiting outside a property. The motor must suit the size, weight and daily use of the gate or door, while safety beams, secure closing and battery backup should be considered from the start. Have unusual noises, slow movement and intermittent operation inspected before a small fault becomes a breakdown.

4. Improve lighting without creating glare

Light the gate, driveway, side passages and external doors. Motion-activated lighting can draw attention to unexpected movement, while carefully positioned fittings avoid blinding cameras or neighbours. Backup or solar lighting can keep important areas visible during power interruptions.

5. Use an alarm as part of the wider system

A monitored alarm is most useful when detection, communication and response all work together. Test sensors and panic buttons, confirm the backup battery is healthy and make sure the monitoring company has current contact details. Cameras can help verify an event, but they should complement rather than replace reliable detection.

A simple place to begin

Walk around the property in daylight and again after dark. List every entrance, blind spot, weak lock, unreliable motor and area where backup power is missing. Prioritise the points that expose people during arrival and departure, then build the rest of the system around those needs.
